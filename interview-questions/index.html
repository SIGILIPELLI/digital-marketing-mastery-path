    Digital Marketing & SEO Interview Questions and Answers¶

    179 questions with the correct answer and a short explanation, organised from beginner (Level 1) to advanced (Level 4). Every question comes from a lesson in the free Digital Marketing & SEO Mastery Path, so you can open the lesson to go deeper.

    Practise, don't just read

    Each lesson also ends with an interactive quiz, and the final quiz tests all four levels. Try to answer each question below before reading the answer.

    Level 1 · Entry¶

    01 · Digital Marketing Fundamentals & the Funnel¶

    Q1. What is a marketing funnel?

    Answer: A model of the stages a person passes through from awareness to purchase and loyalty

    Stages guide which metrics and tactics fit.

    Q2. A funnel has 10,000 visitors, 800 leads and 40 customers. What are the lead rate and lead-to-customer rate (percent)?

    print(800/10000*100, 40/800*100)

    Answer: 8.0 5.0

    Conversion rate = outcomes / the stage before. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q3. Which is a top-of-funnel goal?

    Answer: Awareness: reaching relevant people who do not know you yet

    Bottom of the funnel is about decision and conversion.

    Q4. Why measure each funnel stage separately?

    Answer: To find where people drop off and fix the weakest step

    One blended number hides the problem.

    02 · Audiences, Personas & Customer Journeys¶

    Q5. What is a buyer persona?

    Answer: A research-based profile of a typical customer group: goals, problems, behaviour and objections

    Based on data, not guesses.

    Q6. What should a persona be built from?

    Answer: Interviews, surveys, analytics and sales conversations

    Validate with real customers.

    Q7. What is a customer journey map?

    Answer: The steps a customer takes from first awareness to purchase and beyond, with touchpoints and emotions

    Find friction and opportunities.

    Q8. What does a 'job to be done' describe?

    Answer: The progress a customer wants to make, which explains why they buy

    Focus on motives.

    03 · Websites & Landing Pages That Convert¶

    Q9. What makes a landing page convert?

    Answer: One clear goal, a relevant headline, benefits, proof and a single prominent call to action

    Match the message of the ad or link.

    Q10. Why is message match important?

    Answer: Visitors who click an ad expect the page to continue the same promise

    A mismatch increases bounce.

    Q11. What is a call to action (CTA)?

    Answer: The specific next step you want the visitor to take

    Use clear, action-oriented text.

    Q12. What is social proof?

    Answer: Evidence others trust you: reviews, testimonials, case studies, logos

    Reduces perceived risk.

    04 · How Search Engines Work (SEO Basics)¶

    Q13. What does a search engine do before ranking pages?

    Answer: Crawls pages, indexes their content and then ranks results for each query

    Pages must be crawlable and indexable.

    Q14. What is search intent?

    Answer: The goal behind a query (to learn, to find a site, to compare or to buy)

    Content must match intent.

    Q15. What are organic results?

    Answer: Unpaid listings ranked by relevance and quality

    SEO targets them.

    Q16. What is a SERP?

    Answer: The search engine results page

    Features include snippets and maps.

    05 · Keyword Research¶

    Q17. What is a long-tail keyword?

    Answer: A longer, more specific phrase with lower volume but clearer intent and less competition

    Often converts better.

    Q18. A keyword has 1,000 searches/month and you could rank to get a 5% CTR. How many visits per month is that?

    print(1000*0.05)

    Answer: 50.0

    Estimated visits = volume x expected CTR. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q19. What should you check beyond search volume?

    Answer: Intent, difficulty and the business value of the visitor

    Volume alone misleads.

    Q20. What is keyword difficulty?

    Answer: An estimate of how hard it is to rank, based on competing pages

    Tools estimate it differently.

    06 · On-Page SEO¶

    Q21. Where should the primary keyword usually appear?

    Answer: In the title, a heading, the opening paragraph and the URL naturally

    Write for people first.

    Q22. What does the title tag do?

    Answer: Defines the clickable headline in search results and is a strong relevance signal

    Keep it concise and distinct.

    Q23. What is keyword stuffing?

    Answer: Repeating keywords unnaturally, which harms readability and can be penalised

    Use natural language.

    Q24. Why add descriptive alt text to images?

    Answer: Accessibility and image search context

    Describe the image.

    07 · Social Media Marketing Basics¶

    Q25. What is the best first step in social media marketing?

    Answer: Choose platforms where your audience spends time and set clear goals

    Focus beats breadth.

    Q26. A post reached 20,000 people and got 600 engagements. What is the engagement rate on reach (percent)?

    print(600/20000*100)

    Answer: 3.0

    Engagement rate = engagements / reach. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q27. Why plan a content calendar?

    Answer: Consistency, balance of content types and coordination with campaigns

    Plan and adapt.

    Q28. What is organic vs paid social?

    Answer: Organic is unpaid posting; paid promotes content to chosen audiences

    Combine both.

    08 · Email Marketing Basics¶

    Q29. Why is an email list valuable?

    Answer: You own the channel and can reach interested people directly without platform algorithms

    Build it with permission.

    Q30. An email goes to 5,000 people with 1,100 opens, 220 clicks and 22 purchases. What are open, click-through (of delivered) and click-to-purchase rates?

    print(round(1100/5000*100,1), round(220/5000*100,1), round(22/220*100,1))

    Answer: 22.0 4.4 10.0

    Note that open rates are less reliable since privacy changes; clicks and conversions matter more. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q31. What is double opt-in?

    Answer: The subscriber confirms their address via an email link before being added

    Improves list quality.

    Q32. What law-driven element must marketing emails include?

    Answer: A clear unsubscribe option and sender identification (CAN-SPAM, GDPR and similar)

    Obey regional laws.

    09 · Google Analytics 4 Basics¶

    Q33. What is GA4's data model based on?

    Answer: Events, with parameters, rather than sessions and pageviews only

    Everything is an event.

    Q34. What is a conversion in GA4?

    Answer: An event you mark as key because it matters to the business, such as a purchase or lead

    Called key events.

    Q35. What is the difference between a user and a session?

    Answer: A user is a person (or device); a session is a group of interactions in a time window

    Metrics differ.

    Q36. Why define goals before reading reports?

    Answer: Reports answer questions, so you need to know which questions matter

    Measurement plan.

    10 · Project — Launch Plan for a Local Business¶

    Q37. What does the local business launch plan combine?

    Answer: A website, local SEO, a Google Business Profile, social, email and tracking tied to goals

    Level 1 capstone.

    Q38. A launch budget of 600 is split 50% ads, 30% content, 20% tools. How much goes to content?

    print(600*0.30)

    Answer: 180.0

    Budget split by objectives. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q39. What is a good success metric for a local business?

    Answer: Qualified enquiries, bookings or visits, not just followers

    Tie to revenue.

    Q40. Why set a review rhythm?

    Answer: Weekly or monthly checks let you cut what fails and scale what works

    Iterate.

    Level 2 · Intermediate¶

    01 · Technical SEO¶

    Q41. What is a sitemap for?

    Answer: Helps search engines discover your important URLs

    Submit in Search Console.

    Q42. What does robots.txt control?

    Answer: Which paths crawlers may request (not whether a page is indexed)

    Use noindex to keep pages out of results.

    Q43. What are Core Web Vitals?

    Answer: Measures of loading (LCP), interactivity (INP) and visual stability (CLS)

    A page experience signal.

    Q44. What does a canonical tag do?

    Answer: Tells search engines which URL is the preferred version of duplicate or similar pages

    Consolidates signals.

    02 · Local SEO & Google Business Profile¶

    Q45. What is a Google Business Profile?

    Answer: A free listing that shows your business in Google Maps and local search

    Verify and complete it.

    Q46. What does NAP consistency mean?

    Answer: Your name, address and phone number match everywhere online

    Consistency builds trust.

    Q47. What helps local rankings?

    Answer: Relevance, distance and prominence (reviews, links, citations)

    Respond to reviews.

    Q48. How should you handle negative reviews?

    Answer: Respond politely and offer to resolve it offline

    Visible to prospects.

    03 · Content Marketing Strategy¶

    Q49. What is content marketing?

    Answer: Creating useful content to attract and retain a defined audience, leading to profitable action

    Helpful first.

    Q50. What is a pillar page?

    Answer: A comprehensive page on a core topic that links to related detailed posts

    Topic clusters.

    Q51. A blog post costs 400 to produce and drives 20 leads in a year worth 50 each. What is the first-year ROI (percent)?

    print((20*50-400)/400*100)

    Answer: 150.0

    ROI = (return - cost) / cost. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q52. How do you repurpose content?

    Answer: Turn one asset into several formats (video, social snippets, email) for different channels

    Saves effort.

    04 · Google Ads: Search Campaigns¶

    Q53. What does 'quality score' influence in Google Ads?

    Answer: Ad rank and cost per click through expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience

    Improve relevance to pay less.

    Q54. A campaign spends 500, gets 10,000 impressions, 250 clicks and 20 conversions. What are CTR, CPC and CPA?

    print(250/10000*100, 500/250, 500/20)

    Answer: 2.5 2.0 25.0

    CTR = clicks/impressions; CPC = cost/clicks; CPA = cost/conversions. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q55. What are negative keywords for?

    Answer: Stop ads showing for irrelevant searches, saving budget

    Review the search terms report.

    Q56. What match type gives the most control?

    Answer: Exact match (and phrase)

    Broad needs strong signals.

    05 · Meta Ads (Facebook & Instagram)¶

    Q57. What is the Meta Pixel (or Conversions API) used for?

    Answer: Measuring actions on your site and optimising and retargeting ads

    Respect consent.

    Q58. An ad set spends 300 and generates 12 purchases worth 90 each. What is ROAS?

    print(12*90/300)

    Answer: 3.6

    ROAS = revenue / ad spend. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q59. Why test several creatives?

    Answer: Creative is the largest lever on performance, so find what resonates

    Test one variable at a time.

    Q60. What is a lookalike or similar audience?

    Answer: People modelled to resemble your existing customers or seed list

    Needs a good seed.

    06 · YouTube & Video Marketing¶

    Q61. Why is YouTube a search engine as well as a social platform?

    Answer: People search for answers and tutorials there, so titles and descriptions matter

    Optimise for discovery.

    Q62. What signals matter for video performance?

    Answer: Click-through on thumbnails and titles, and watch time or retention

    Hook early.

    Q63. What is a hook?

    Answer: The first seconds that give viewers a reason to keep watching

    Keep promises.

    Q64. How can you use video in a funnel?

    Answer: Educational videos for awareness, demos and testimonials for consideration, offers for conversion

    Match stage.

    07 · Email Automation & Segmentation¶

    Q65. What is email segmentation?

    Answer: Dividing subscribers into groups by attributes or behaviour so messages are relevant

    Relevance increases results.

    Q66. What is an automated welcome series?

    Answer: A set of emails triggered when someone subscribes, introducing you and guiding next steps

    High engagement.

    Q67. What triggers an abandoned-cart email?

    Answer: A shopper adds items to the cart and leaves without purchasing

    A timely reminder.

    Q68. Why clean your list?

    Answer: Remove inactive or invalid addresses to protect deliverability

    Better inbox placement.

    08 · Conversion Tracking: GA4 Events & UTMs¶

    Q69. What do UTM parameters do?

    Answer: Tag URLs so analytics can attribute traffic to source, medium and campaign

    utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign.

    Q70. Parse this campaign URL's UTM parameters.

    from urllib.parse import urlparse, parse_qs
u='https://example.com/offer?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=spring_sale'
q=parse_qs(urlparse(u).query)
print({k:v[0] for k,v in q.items()})

    Answer: {'utm_source': 'newsletter', 'utm_medium': 'email', 'utm_campaign': 'spring_sale'}

    Use consistent, lowercase naming. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q71. Why agree a naming convention for UTMs?

    Answer: Inconsistent names fragment reports

    Document it.

    Q72. What is a GA4 event with parameters?

    Answer: A recorded action (such as generate_lead) with details like form name or value

    Define a measurement plan.

    09 · Conversion Rate Optimization Basics¶

    Q73. What does conversion rate optimisation (CRO) do?

    Answer: Systematically improves the share of visitors who take a desired action

    Research, hypothesise, test.

    Q74. A page converts 2% of 5,000 visitors. After a change it converts 2.6% of 5,000. How many extra conversions?

    print(round(5000*0.026-5000*0.02))

    Answer: 30

    Compare outcomes at equal traffic. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q75. What should you do before changing a page?

    Answer: Study analytics, recordings and user feedback to form hypotheses

    Data-driven.

    Q76. What is a common cause of low conversion?

    Answer: Unclear value proposition or too much friction in forms and navigation

    Simplify.

    10 · Project — A Small-Budget Lead Generation Campaign¶

    Q77. What does the lead-generation campaign project combine?

    Answer: An offer, landing page, ads, tracking and follow-up with a small budget

    Level 2 capstone.

    Q78. With a 900 budget and a 15 cost per lead, how many leads can you expect?

    print(900/15)

    Answer: 60.0

    Leads = budget / CPL. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q79. If 10% of leads become customers worth 400 each from 60 leads, what is the revenue and the revenue per lead?

    print(60*0.10*400, 60*0.10*400/60)

    Answer: 2400.0 40.0

    Compare to the cost per lead to see profitability. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q80. What do you do with results after two weeks?

    Answer: Pause weak ads, shift budget to winners and iterate on creative

    Optimise fast.

    Level 3 · Advanced¶

    01 · Link Building & Digital PR¶

    Q81. What is link building?

    Answer: Earning links from other reputable sites to yours to build authority

    Quality and relevance matter.

    Q82. Why are paid or manipulative links risky?

    Answer: They violate search guidelines and can cause penalties

    Earn links with value.

    Q83. What is digital PR?

    Answer: Creating newsworthy content that earns coverage and links from publications

    Data-led stories work well.

    Q84. What is a good link-prospect check?

    Answer: Relevance, audience fit and the site's own quality

    Not just authority scores.

    02 · Topic Clusters & Content at Scale¶

    Q85. What are topic clusters?

    Answer: A pillar page and related articles interlinked to cover a subject comprehensively

    Signals topical authority.

    Q86. What is a content brief?

    Answer: A guide for writers covering intent, outline, keywords, sources and CTA

    Keeps quality consistent.

    Q87. How do you scale content without losing quality?

    Answer: Templates, editorial standards, subject-matter expert review and measuring outcomes

    Quality control.

    Q88. What is content decay and the fix?

    Answer: Traffic falling as content ages; update, consolidate or prune it

    Refresh regularly.

    03 · Performance Max, Shopping & Remarketing¶

    Q89. What is Performance Max?

    Answer: A Google Ads campaign type using automation to serve ads across Google channels from your assets and goals

    Feed it good signals.

    Q90. What does a product feed do for Shopping ads?

    Answer: Provides product data (title, price, image, availability) that powers the ads

    Feed quality drives results.

    Q91. What is remarketing?

    Answer: Showing ads to people who have already visited your site or engaged

    Respect consent and limit frequency.

    Q92. A shopping campaign spends 1,000 with ROAS 4. What revenue does it generate? And at a 25% margin what is profit before other costs?

    rev=1000*4
print(rev, rev*0.25-1000)

    Answer: 4000 0.0

    A 4x ROAS only breaks even at a 25% margin; profitability needs margin, not just ROAS. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    04 · Marketing Dashboards with Looker Studio¶

    Q93. What is a good dashboard principle?

    Answer: Show the few metrics tied to decisions, with context such as targets and trends

    Answer questions.

    Q94. What does Looker Studio do?

    Answer: Connects data sources and builds shareable dashboards and reports

    Free Google tool.

    Q95. Why show targets next to metrics?

    Answer: Context tells viewers whether performance is good or bad

    Add comparisons.

    Q96. What is a data-quality risk with dashboards?

    Answer: Mismatched definitions or broken connections producing wrong numbers

    Validate against the source.

    05 · A/B Testing & Experimentation¶

    Q97. Variant A: 200 conversions of 5,000 visitors. Variant B: 250 of 5,000. What is B's relative uplift and is the difference significant at 95% (z-test)?

    from math import sqrt
a,b,n=200,250,5000
pa,pb=a/n,b/n
p=(a+b)/(2*n)
z=(pb-pa)/sqrt(p*(1-p)*(2/n))
print(round((pb-pa)/pa*100), round(z,2), abs(z)>1.96)

    Answer: 25 2.41 True

    z = difference / standard error; |z| above 1.96 is significant at 95%. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q98. Why decide the sample size and duration before a test?

    Answer: Stopping early when results look good inflates false positives

    Avoid peeking.

    Q99. What should you test first?

    Answer: The change with the biggest likely impact, with a clear hypothesis

    Prioritise.

    Q100. What does statistical significance not tell you?

    Answer: Whether the effect is large enough to matter for the business

    Look at effect size.

    06 · Marketing Automation & CRM¶

    Q101. What is marketing automation?

    Answer: Software that sends targeted messages and updates records based on behaviour and rules

    Workflows.

    Q102. What is lead scoring?

    Answer: Assigning points to leads by fit and behaviour to prioritise follow-up

    Align with sales.

    Q103. What does a CRM do?

    Answer: Stores contacts and interactions and tracks deals through a pipeline

    Single source of truth.

    Q104. What is an MQL?

    Answer: A marketing-qualified lead who meets criteria for handoff to sales

    Agree definitions.

    07 · Influencer & Affiliate Marketing¶

    Q105. What is an affiliate programme?

    Answer: Partners earn a commission for sales or leads they send you

    Performance-based.

    Q106. What should influencer deals include?

    Answer: Clear deliverables, disclosure of the partnership, usage rights and measurement

    Follow disclosure rules.

    Q107. An influencer post costs 1,500 and drives 60 sales at 80 average order value with 40% gross margin. What is the profit contribution after the fee?

    print(60*80*0.40-1500)

    Answer: 420.0

    Judge by margin, not revenue. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q108. How do you track affiliate or influencer sales?

    Answer: Unique links or codes

    Attribution.

    08 · E-commerce Marketing¶

    Q109. What are key e-commerce marketing metrics?

    Answer: Conversion rate, average order value, customer acquisition cost and repeat purchase rate

    Revenue levers.

    Q110. An order value is 60 with 25% margin and CAC is 18. Is the first order profitable?

    print(60*0.25-18)

    Answer: -3.0

    First orders can lose money; repeat purchases make up for it if retention is real. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q111. What is a cart abandonment email for?

    Answer: Recover shoppers who left items behind

    Time it well.

    Q112. What drives repeat purchases?

    Answer: Great product experience, post-purchase email and relevant offers

    Retention lowers CAC.

    09 · AI Tools for Marketers¶

    Q113. How should marketers use AI tools?

    Answer: As assistants for drafting, analysis and ideas, with human review for accuracy, brand and ethics

    Check facts.

    Q114. What is a risk of AI-generated content?

    Answer: Inaccuracy and generic text that fails to add value or breaks guidelines

    Edit and add expertise.

    Q115. What data should you not paste into public AI tools?

    Answer: Confidential customer or company information

    Follow policy.

    Q116. What is prompt clarity's benefit?

    Answer: Better outputs with context, constraints and examples

    Iterate.

    10 · Project — Full-Funnel Growth Plan for an Online Store¶

    Q117. What does the full-funnel plan combine?

    Answer: Acquisition, conversion, retention and measurement for an online store with budgets

    Level 3 capstone.

    Q118. A store has 50,000 monthly visitors, 2% conversion and 70 average order value. What is monthly revenue?

    print(50000*0.02*70)

    Answer: 70000.0

    Revenue = traffic x conversion x AOV. (Illustrative numbers; real campaign results vary.) Verified by running it.

    Q119. Raising conversion from 2% to 2.5% at the same traffic and AOV adds how much monthly revenue?

    print(50000*0.005*70)

    Answer: 17500.0

from urllib.parse import urlparse, parse_qs

# illustrative campaign data: (landing URL with UTMs, spend, impressions, clicks, conversions, revenue)
rows = [
    ("https://shop.example/a?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=brand", 400, 20000, 1400, 70, 5600),
    ("https://shop.example/a?utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=generic", 900, 90000, 1800, 45, 3150),
    ("https://shop.example/b?utm_source=meta&utm_medium=paid_social&utm_campaign=lookalike", 600, 150000, 1500, 36, 3240),
    ("https://shop.example/c?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=spring", 50, 8000, 640, 48, 4320),
    ("https://shop.example/d?utm_source=influencer&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=creator1", 300, 60000, 500, 6, 480),
]

def campaign(url):
    q = parse_qs(urlparse(url).query)
    return q["utm_source"][0], q["utm_medium"][0], q["utm_campaign"][0]

def metrics(spend, impr, clicks, conv, revenue):
    return {
        "ctr": 100 * clicks / impr,
        "cpc": spend / clicks,
        "cvr": 100 * conv / clicks,
        "cpa": spend / conv,
        "roas": revenue / spend,
    }

report = []
for url, *vals in rows:
    src, med, camp = campaign(url)
    m = metrics(*vals)
    report.append((camp, src, med, vals[0], m))

print(f"{'campaign':10} {'ctr%':>5} {'cpc':>5} {'cvr%':>5} {'cpa':>6} {'roas':>5}  action")
for camp, src, med, spend, m in sorted(report, key=lambda r: -r[4]["roas"]):
    action = "scale" if m["roas"] >= 4 else "keep" if m["roas"] >= 2 else "fix or pause"
    print(f"{camp:10} {m['ctr']:5.2f} {m['cpc']:5.2f} {m['cvr']:5.1f} {m['cpa']:6.2f} {m['roas']:5.2f}  {action}")

total_spend = sum(r[3] for r in report)
total_rev = sum(v[-1] for _, *v in rows)
print(f"blended ROAS {total_rev / total_spend:.2f} on spend {total_spend}")

# reallocate the same total budget in proportion to ROAS, capping any campaign at +/-50% of its current spend
roas = {r[0]: r[4]["roas"] for r in report}
spend = {r[0]: r[3] for r in report}
weights = {c: roas[c] for c in roas}
raw = {c: total_spend * weights[c] / sum(weights.values()) for c in roas}
capped = {c: min(max(raw[c], spend[c] * 0.5), spend[c] * 1.5) for c in roas}
scale = total_spend / sum(capped.values())
final = {c: round(v * scale) for c, v in capped.items()}
print("proposed budget:", dict(sorted(final.items())), "total", sum(final.values()))
channels = {}
for camp, src, med, sp, m in report:
    channels[med] = channels.get(med, 0) + sp
print("spend by medium:", dict(sorted(channels.items())))
```

Dividing by the right base matters (conversion rate per click, CPA per conversion). Proportional-to-ROAS reallocation with caps limits how far one noisy week can swing spend, and the final rescale keeps the total equal. Verified by running the Python solution.

from math import sqrt, ceil

def z_test(conv_a, n_a, conv_b, n_b):
    pa, pb = conv_a / n_a, conv_b / n_b
    p = (conv_a + conv_b) / (n_a + n_b)
    se = sqrt(p * (1 - p) * (1 / n_a + 1 / n_b))
    return pa, pb, (pb - pa) / se

def sample_size(base, lift, z_alpha=1.96, z_beta=0.84):
    """visitors per variant to detect a relative lift (two-sided 5% alpha, 80% power)"""
    p1, p2 = base, base * (1 + lift)
    pbar = (p1 + p2) / 2
    return ceil(((z_alpha * sqrt(2 * pbar * (1 - pbar)) + z_beta * sqrt(p1 * (1 - p1) + p2 * (1 - p2))) ** 2) / (p2 - p1) ** 2)

for name, (ca, na, cb, nb) in {"headline test": (200, 5000, 250, 5000), "button colour": (180, 4000, 190, 4000)}.items():
    pa, pb, z = z_test(ca, na, cb, nb)
    verdict = "significant" if abs(z) > 1.96 else "not significant"
    print(f"{name}: {100 * pa:.1f}% vs {100 * pb:.1f}% (lift {100 * (pb - pa) / pa:+.0f}%), z={z:.2f}, {verdict}")
print("visitors per variant to detect +10% on a 3% base:", sample_size(0.03, 0.10))
print("visitors per variant to detect +25% on a 3% base:", sample_size(0.03, 0.25))

# attribution: one journey, 100 of revenue
journey = ["search", "email", "social", "search", "email"]
def attribute(path, model, value=100.0):
    n = len(path)
    if model == "first":
        w = [1] + [0] * (n - 1)
    elif model == "last":
        w = [0] * (n - 1) + [1]
    elif model == "linear":
        w = [1 / n] * n
    elif model == "position":
        w = [0.4] + [0.2 / (n - 2)] * (n - 2) + [0.4] if n > 2 else [0.5] * n
    elif model == "time_decay":
        raw = [0.5 ** (n - 1 - i) for i in range(n)]
        w = [r / sum(raw) for r in raw]
    credit = {}
    for ch, wi in zip(path, w):
        credit[ch] = credit.get(ch, 0) + wi * value
    return {k: round(v, 1) for k, v in sorted(credit.items())}
for m in ["first", "last", "linear", "position", "time_decay"]:
    print(f"{m:10}", attribute(journey, m))
```

The headline test clears the 1.96 threshold, the button test does not, and detecting a small lift needs far more traffic than a large one. Each attribution model tells a different story about the same journey, which is why you compare them and test incrementality. Verified by running the Python solution.

# 01 · Digital Marketing Fundamentals & the Funnel

Digital marketing is the work of getting the right people to notice a business online, trust it, and take an action — buy, call, sign up or visit. This module gives you the map every later lesson hangs on: the **funnel**, the **channels** that feed it, and the **numbers** that tell you whether it works. We'll use one running example through Level 1: *Chai Corner*, a small café in Pune that wants more weekday customers and catering orders.

## 1. The marketing funnel

1. **Awareness** — people discover you exist: a Google search result, an Instagram Reel, a friend's WhatsApp forward.
2. **Consideration** — they compare: read reviews, check your menu, look at prices and photos.
3. **Conversion** — they act: order online, call, walk in, fill a catering enquiry form.
4. **Retention & advocacy** — they come back and recommend you: loyalty offers, email or WhatsApp updates, review requests.
5. Each stage loses people. If 10,000 people see Chai Corner's posts, 400 visit the website (4%), and 20 send an enquiry (5% of visitors), the funnel converts 0.2% overall. Improving any one stage lifts the final number.

## 2. The main channels

1. **Search (SEO)** — free, slow-building traffic from Google when people search things like "café near FC Road".
2. **Paid search (Google Ads)** — pay per click to appear for those searches immediately.
3. **Social media (organic and paid)** — Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, LinkedIn; great for awareness and community.
4. **Email and WhatsApp** — direct, cheap retention channels you own.
5. **Content** — blogs, videos and guides that answer questions and earn trust (and feed SEO and social).
6. **Referral and partnerships** — influencers, affiliates, local collaborations.
7. Owned (website, email list), earned (reviews, press, shares) and paid (ads) media work together — paid ads can kick-start awareness while owned channels build long-term value.

## 3. The numbers that matter

1. **Impressions / reach** — how many times / people saw something.
2. **CTR (click-through rate)** = clicks ÷ impressions. 150 clicks from 5,000 impressions = 3%.
3. **Conversion rate** = conversions ÷ visitors. 12 enquiries from 300 visitors = 4%.
4. **CPC, CPL, CPA** — cost per click, per lead, per acquisition. ₹3,000 spend ÷ 12 leads = ₹250 CPL.
5. **ROAS** = revenue ÷ ad spend. ₹18,000 revenue from ₹3,000 spend = 6× ROAS.
6. **CLV (customer lifetime value)** — what a customer is worth over time. A regular who spends ₹200 twice a week for a year is worth far more than one visit, which justifies a higher acquisition cost.

## 4. Setting a SMART goal

1. **Specific** — "increase weekday catering enquiries", not "grow the brand".
2. **Measurable** — a number you can track: 30 enquiries a month.
3. **Achievable** — based on current numbers (10 a month today).
4. **Relevant** — tied to revenue: catering orders average ₹6,000.
5. **Time-bound** — within 90 days.
6. Write it in one line: *"Grow catering enquiries from 10 to 30 per month by 31 March, at a cost per lead under ₹300."*

## How It Actually Works

Every digital channel is a matching system: platforms like Google and Meta are trying to show each person the content most likely to keep them satisfied, and they charge advertisers or reward publishers accordingly. That's why relevance beats volume — a page or ad that closely matches what someone wants earns higher click-through rates, which platforms reward with better rankings or cheaper clicks. The funnel is simply a way to see where that matching breaks: low CTR means your message doesn't fit the audience, a low conversion rate means the page doesn't deliver what the click promised, and weak retention means the product or follow-up isn't earning a second visit. Marketing improves results by fixing the narrowest stage first.

# 10 · Project — Launch Plan for a Local Business

Time to combine everything from Level 1 into one deliverable: a 90-day digital marketing launch plan for a real local business. This is the kind of document freelancers and junior marketers present to clients, so make it good enough to show.

## 1. Choose the business and goal

1. Pick a real local business (a café, coaching centre, clinic, salon, boutique, repair shop). If possible, work with the owner.
2. Write a **SMART goal** (Module 1): e.g. "Get 40 new trial-class enquiries per month for a dance academy in Kochi within 90 days at under ₹250 per lead."
3. Record the **current baseline**: monthly enquiries, website visits, followers, Google reviews.

## 2. Audience and message

1. One **primary persona** and one secondary (Module 2), each with goals, pains, channels, objections.
2. A short **journey map** with the three biggest drop-off risks.
3. The **value proposition** sentence and a headline.

## 3. Website and SEO plan

1. Audit the current site with the five-second checklist and PageSpeed (Module 3). List the top five fixes.
2. Outline one **landing page** for the main offer: headline, benefits, proof, FAQ, CTA.
3. A **keyword map** of 15+ keywords across 4–5 pages (Module 5).
4. Title tags, meta descriptions and H1s for the three most important pages (Module 6).
5. Confirm Search Console is set up and the sitemap submitted (Module 4).

## 4. Social and email plan

1. Two chosen social platforms and why (Module 7).
2. Four content pillars and a **four-week content calendar** (12+ posts, Story ideas, two Reel scripts).
3. A lead magnet, sign-up form placement and a **welcome email** plus two newsletters (Module 8).

## 5. Measurement

1. GA4 installed, with a key event for enquiries (Module 9).
2. A **weekly scorecard**: sessions by channel, enquiries, conversion rate, social reach, email sign-ups.
3. A 90-day timeline: Weeks 1–2 fixes and setup; Weeks 3–8 content and SEO; Weeks 9–12 review, double down on what works.

## How It Actually Works

A good launch plan is a chain of assumptions you can test: *this persona searches these keywords, this page converts them at roughly this rate, this channel reaches them at this cost*. Writing the chain down — with a baseline and a scorecard — is what turns marketing from guesswork into a system you can improve each week. Clients and employers value the plan less for its predictions than for showing that you know what to measure and how you'll adjust.

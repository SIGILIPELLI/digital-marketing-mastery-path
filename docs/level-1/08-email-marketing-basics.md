# 08 · Email Marketing Basics

Email is the channel you **own**: no algorithm decides whether your subscribers see your message. Done well, it's the cheapest way to turn interested people into customers and customers into regulars. This module covers building a list, writing emails people open, and measuring results.

## 1. Build a permission-based list

1. Only email people who opted in. Buying lists hurts deliverability and breaks laws such as India's **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** and anti-spam rules in other countries.
2. Offer a reason to subscribe (a **lead magnet**): "Office snack menu + price list PDF", "10% off your first catering order", a monthly recipe.
3. Add sign-up forms to the website, landing page and thank-you page; collect only name and email (and consent).
4. Use an email platform: **Mailchimp**, **Brevo**, **MailerLite**, **Zoho Campaigns** or **ConvertKit** — all have free tiers.

## 2. Write emails that get opened and clicked

1. **Sender name** people recognise: "Neha at Chai Corner" often beats "info".
2. **Subject line** (30–50 characters): specific and useful — "This Friday's office snack menu (from ₹120)".
3. **Preview text**: the line shown after the subject; use it to add a second reason to open.
4. **One main message and one CTA** per email: "Book Friday's order".
5. Short paragraphs, a clear button, mobile-friendly layout (most emails are read on phones).
6. Always include your address and an easy **unsubscribe** link.

## 3. Types of email

1. **Welcome email** — sent immediately after sign-up; deliver the lead magnet, set expectations, highest open rates you'll get.
2. **Newsletter** — regular value plus a light offer.
3. **Promotional** — a time-bound offer or launch.
4. **Transactional** — order confirmations, invoices; reliable and expected.
5. **Re-engagement** — "Still want our menus?" to inactive subscribers; remove those who never open to protect deliverability.

## 4. Measure and improve

1. **Open rate** — opens ÷ delivered. Apple's Mail Privacy Protection inflates opens, so treat it as a rough signal.
2. **Click-through rate (CTR)** — clicks ÷ delivered; a more reliable engagement metric.
3. **Conversion rate** — orders or bookings from the email (track with UTM links — Level 2, Module 8).
4. **Unsubscribe and spam complaint rates** — keep complaints well under 0.1%.
5. **A/B test** one variable at a time, such as two subject lines on a sample before sending the winner to everyone.

## Cheat sheet

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Permission | Opt-in only; consent recorded |
| Lead magnet | Useful freebie or discount |
| Subject | 30–50 chars, specific |
| Preview text | Second reason to open |
| Body | One message, one CTA, mobile-first |
| Footer | Address + unsubscribe |
| Metrics | CTR, conversions, unsubscribes, complaints |

## How It Actually Works

Inbox providers like Gmail decide whether your email lands in the primary tab, promotions or spam based on **sender reputation**: authentication records on your domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), how many recipients open and engage, and how many delete without reading or mark you as spam. Sending relevant emails to people who asked for them builds reputation; blasting cold or inactive addresses destroys it. Gmail and Yahoo now require bulk senders to authenticate their domain and provide one-click unsubscribe, so setting up your platform's domain authentication is part of email marketing, not an optional extra.

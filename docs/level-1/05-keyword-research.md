# 05 · Keyword Research

Keywords are the words people type into search engines. Keyword research tells you what your audience is actually searching for, how often, and how hard it is to rank — so you create pages and ads for real demand instead of guessing.

## 1. Brainstorm seed keywords

1. Start from your persona's problems and the products you sell: "office catering", "snacks for meetings", "café Baner", "chai delivery".
2. Add modifiers people use: location ("in Pune", "near me"), price ("cheap", "per plate"), quality ("best"), format ("for 50 people").
3. Use Google itself: type a seed and note **autocomplete** suggestions, **People also ask** questions and **related searches** at the bottom.

## 2. Get volumes and ideas

1. **Google Keyword Planner** (free inside a Google Ads account: **Tools → Keyword Planner → Discover new keywords**). Enter seeds and location (e.g. Pune); it shows average monthly searches as ranges and top-of-page bid estimates.
2. **Google Search Console → Performance** shows queries you already appear for — gold for existing sites.
3. **Google Trends** compares interest over time and seasonality (e.g. "diwali gift hampers" peaks in October).
4. Paid tools such as Ahrefs, Semrush or Ubersuggest add difficulty scores and competitor keywords; free tiers are fine for practice.

## 3. Judge each keyword

1. **Volume** — how many monthly searches. Local keywords are small (50–500) but valuable.
2. **Intent** — informational, commercial, transactional or local (Module 4).
3. **Difficulty** — how strong the pages already ranking are. Search the keyword: if the top results are big brands and directories with strong sites, it's hard for a new site.
4. **Business value** — would this searcher buy? "office catering Baner" (20 searches, ready to buy) beats "chai recipe" (50,000 searches, won't buy catering).
5. Favour **long-tail keywords** (3+ words, specific) when starting out — lower competition, clearer intent.

## 4. Organise into a keyword map

1. Group keywords with the same intent into a **cluster**: "office catering Pune", "corporate snacks Pune", "team lunch catering Pune" → one page.
2. Assign **one primary keyword** per page and 3–6 secondary ones. Don't make two pages compete for the same keyword.
3. Build a simple sheet:

| Page | Primary keyword | Secondary keywords | Intent | Monthly volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| /office-catering | office catering pune | corporate snacks pune, team lunch catering | Transactional | 210 |
| /menu | café menu baner | chai and snacks baner, café prices | Local | 90 |
| /blog/office-party-snacks | office party snack ideas | snacks for team meeting | Informational | 480 |

## Cheat sheet

| Source | Use |
|---|---|
| Autocomplete / People also ask | Real phrasing and questions |
| Keyword Planner | Volume ranges, bid estimates |
| Search Console | Queries you already rank for |
| Google Trends | Seasonality |
| Long-tail | Specific, easier, higher intent |
| Keyword map | One primary keyword per page |

## How It Actually Works

Search volume numbers are estimates averaged over months and grouped into ranges, so treat them as relative, not exact. What matters more is the match between a keyword's intent and the page you create: Google tries to understand the meaning behind a query and groups similar phrasings together, which is why one well-built page can rank for dozens of related long-tail searches. Mapping one primary keyword per page prevents **keyword cannibalisation**, where two of your own pages split relevance signals and both rank worse.

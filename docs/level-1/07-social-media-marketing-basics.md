# 07 · Social Media Marketing Basics

Social media is where people discover brands, build trust and talk back. This module shows you how to choose platforms, plan content that people actually engage with, and measure it — without burning out.

## 1. Choose platforms by audience

1. **Instagram** — visual, Reels-driven; strong for food, fashion, lifestyle, local businesses and creators.
2. **Facebook** — older audiences, local groups, events; still powerful for ads.
3. **YouTube** — search-driven video, tutorials, long-form trust (Level 2, Module 6).
4. **LinkedIn** — B2B, professionals, hiring; ideal for Chai Corner's office catering persona.
5. **WhatsApp Business** — catalogue, quick replies, broadcast lists, Status; essential in India for conversations.
6. Start with **two** platforms where your persona already spends time. Doing two well beats five badly.

## 2. Set up profiles properly

1. Consistent name, logo and handle across platforms.
2. Bio: what you offer, for whom, location, and a CTA with a link (website or WhatsApp).
3. Switch Instagram to a **Professional (Business) account** for insights and contact buttons.
4. Fill in hours, address, categories and highlight stories (Menu, Reviews, Catering, How to order).

## 3. Plan content with pillars

1. Pick 3–5 **content pillars** tied to your persona: *Behind the scenes* (making samosas at 7 am), *Proof* (office orders delivered, reviews), *Value* (snack ideas for meetings), *Offers* (weekday combo), *Community* (regular customers, local events).
2. Mix formats: Reels/short video (highest reach), carousels (saves and shares), single images, Stories (daily touchpoints, polls), Lives.
3. Use a simple monthly **content calendar**: date, pillar, format, caption idea, CTA, status.
4. A realistic rhythm for a small business: 3–4 posts a week plus daily Stories.
5. Hooks matter: the first 1–2 seconds of a Reel and the first line of a caption decide whether people stop scrolling.

## 4. Engage and measure

1. Reply to comments and DMs quickly — engagement in the first hour helps reach.
2. Use relevant hashtags (a handful, specific: #PuneFood #BanerCafe) and location tags.
3. Collaborate: Instagram **Collab** posts with local creators or partner businesses.
4. Measure weekly in Insights: reach, engagement rate (interactions ÷ reach), saves, shares, profile visits, link clicks, and messages that turn into orders.
5. Double down on formats and topics that earn saves and shares; drop what doesn't perform after a fair test (4–6 posts).

## Cheat sheet

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Platforms | Start with 2 where your persona is |
| Bio | Offer + audience + location + CTA link |
| Pillars | 3–5 recurring themes |
| Formats | Reels for reach, carousels for saves, Stories daily |
| Engagement rate | Interactions ÷ reach |
| Cadence | 3–4 posts/week + Stories |
| Review | Weekly insights; keep what's saved/shared |

## How It Actually Works

Social feeds are ranked by recommendation systems that predict how likely each person is to watch, like, comment, share or save a post. Early signals — watch time in the first seconds, shares to friends, saves — tell the system the content is worth showing to more people, including non-followers. That's why hooks and genuinely useful or entertaining content outperform polished ads: the algorithm distributes what people engage with, not what brands want to say.

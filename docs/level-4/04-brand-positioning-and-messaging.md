# 04 · Brand Positioning & Messaging

Performance marketing captures demand; **brand** creates it and makes every channel cheaper. A clear position — what you stand for, for whom, against whom — makes ads convert better, lets you charge more, and makes people search for you by name. This module builds positioning and a messaging framework.

## 1. Positioning foundations

1. **Target customer** — the segment you serve best (from your personas).
2. **Market category** — the frame of reference customers use ("office caterer", not "food company").
3. **Competitive alternatives** — what customers would do if you didn't exist (aggregators, in-house pantry, another café).
4. **Unique attributes** — capabilities alternatives lack (2-hour delivery guarantee, GST-compliant billing, 100% fresh, hot boxes).
5. **Value** — what those attributes mean for the customer (no awkward meetings with cold food, no billing headaches).
6. Positioning statement: *For [target] who [need], [brand] is the [category] that [value], because [proof], unlike [alternative].*

## 2. Messaging hierarchy

1. **Brand promise** (one line): "Office food that arrives hot, on time, every time."
2. **3–4 message pillars** with proof: Reliability (on-time rate, guarantee), Quality (fresh daily, chef background), Ease (WhatsApp ordering, GST invoices), Value (per-head pricing).
3. **Proof points** for each: numbers, testimonials, certifications, case studies.
4. **Tone of voice**: e.g. warm, confident, plain-spoken; with examples of words to use and avoid.
5. Adapt by channel and stage: ads lead with one pillar; sales decks cover all.

## 3. Brand identity and consistency

1. Visual identity: logo, colours, typography, photography style — used consistently across website, social, packaging, ads.
2. Distinctive brand assets: a recognisable colour, character, sound or tagline repeated over time builds memory.
3. A one-page **brand guidelines** document so freelancers and agencies stay consistent.

## 4. Measure brand

1. **Branded search volume** (Search Console, Google Trends) — are more people searching your name?
2. **Direct traffic** and returning visitors.
3. **Share of voice** — your visibility vs competitors in search, social and ads.
4. Simple brand surveys: awareness ("Which office caterers in Pune have you heard of?") and consideration.
5. Reviews and sentiment over time.

## Cheat sheet

| Element | Output |
|---|---|
| Positioning | Target, category, alternatives, attributes, value |
| Brand promise | One line |
| Message pillars | 3–4 with proof points |
| Tone of voice | Traits + do/don't words |
| Identity | Logo, colours, type, imagery |
| Brand metrics | Branded search, direct traffic, share of voice |

## How It Actually Works

Buying decisions rely heavily on memory: when a need arises, people consider the brands that come to mind easily. Consistent, distinctive messaging over time builds those memory links, which is why brand-building lifts the performance of every direct-response channel — people click and convert more readily on names they recognise. Positioning sharpens this by giving the brain one simple reason to choose you, rather than a list of features that blur with competitors.

# 05 · Growth Marketing Systems

Growth marketing treats growth as a repeatable process: find the levers across the whole customer lifecycle, run many fast experiments, and scale what works. This module builds the operating system — north star metric, growth model, experiment pipeline and team rhythm.

## 1. North star metric and inputs

1. A **north star metric** captures the value customers get and predicts revenue: weekly active learners, monthly repeat orders, paid subscribers.
2. Break it into **input metrics** teams can influence: new sign-ups, activation rate, purchase frequency, retention.
3. Example for a meal subscription: North star = active weekly subscribers; inputs = trials started, trial-to-paid %, weekly churn.

## 2. Map the growth model (AARRR)

1. **Acquisition** — people arrive (channels, CAC).
2. **Activation** — first value moment (first order delivered, first lesson completed).
3. **Retention** — they come back.
4. **Referral** — they invite others.
5. **Revenue** — they pay, and pay more over time.
6. Find the biggest leak with funnel and **cohort analysis** (do customers acquired in March retain better than in April?). Often activation and retention fixes beat new acquisition.

## 3. Experiment pipeline

1. Collect ideas from data, customers, sales and competitors in a backlog.
2. Score with **ICE** or **RICE** (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort).
3. Write each as a test: hypothesis, metric, minimum detectable effect, duration (Level 3, Module 5).
4. Run a weekly cadence: review last week's results, decide (scale, iterate, kill), launch new tests.
5. Aim for velocity with rigour: several tests per month, each properly measured.

## 4. Growth loops and team rhythm

1. **Loops** compound: user-generated content attracts new users who create more content; referrals bring customers who refer others; content ranks, earns links, ranks more.
2. Design for loops, not just funnels: what action by a customer brings the next customer?
3. Weekly growth meeting (30–45 minutes): metrics, learnings, next experiments.
4. Share a learnings library so the whole company benefits.

## How It Actually Works

Growth compounds when improvements multiply rather than add: a 10% better activation rate raises retention, referral and revenue from every future cohort. Cohort analysis reveals whether gains are real or just a temporary lift, and a disciplined experiment pipeline turns uncertainty into knowledge at a steady rate. Loops matter because they make growth partly self-sustaining — each customer or piece of content contributes to acquiring the next — reducing dependence on paid media.

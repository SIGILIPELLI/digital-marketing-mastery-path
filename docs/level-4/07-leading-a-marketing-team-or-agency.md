# 07 · Leading a Marketing Team or Agency

Senior marketers deliver results through other people — in-house teams, freelancers and agencies. This module covers structuring a team, managing agencies, setting goals and running the operating rhythm.

## 1. Structure the team

1. Early stage: a generalist marketer plus freelancers (design, content, ads) and tools.
2. Growing: specialists by channel (SEO/content, performance, lifecycle/CRM, brand/social) with a shared analytics function.
3. Mature: teams aligned to goals or customer segments (acquisition, retention, enterprise), supported by shared services (creative, analytics, marketing ops).
4. Decide what to keep **in-house** (strategy, brand, customer insight, data) and what to **outsource** (specialist execution, overflow, new channels to test).

## 2. Hire and develop

1. Write role scorecards: outcomes expected in 6–12 months, not just task lists.
2. Use practical assessments: audit a real landing page, plan a campaign, critique an ad account.
3. Onboard with access, documented processes, a 30/60/90-day plan and early wins.
4. Weekly 1:1s focused on priorities, blockers and growth; give specific, timely feedback.

## 3. Work with agencies and freelancers

1. Brief clearly: goals, KPIs, budget, audience, brand guidelines, constraints, timeline.
2. Agree reporting and access: **you own** the ad accounts, analytics, domains and data; agencies get user access.
3. Pricing models: retainer, percentage of spend, project fee, or performance-linked components — be wary of incentives that reward spending more rather than performing better.
4. Monthly performance reviews against KPIs; quarterly strategic reviews.
5. Run a structured agency selection: shortlist, briefing, pitches with your data, reference checks, trial period.

## 4. Operating rhythm and goals

1. Set **OKRs** (Objectives and Key Results) or clear quarterly targets tied to business goals.
2. Weekly team meeting: metrics, priorities, blockers; monthly business review with leadership.
3. Shared tools: project management (Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Jira), documented processes, a single dashboard.
4. Protect focus: say no to low-value requests by referring to agreed priorities.
5. Celebrate wins and share learnings openly, including failed experiments.

## Cheat sheet

| Area | Practice |
|---|---|
| Structure | Generalist → channel specialists → goal-aligned teams |
| In-house vs outsource | Keep strategy, brand, data in-house |
| Hiring | Scorecards + practical tests |
| Agencies | Clear brief, you own accounts, KPI reviews |
| Goals | OKRs tied to business goals |
| Rhythm | Weekly team, monthly business review |

## How It Actually Works

Team output depends less on individual brilliance than on clarity: shared goals, defined ownership, reliable data and a regular decision rhythm. Ownership of accounts and data protects the business's accumulated learning — ad account history and conversion data have real value — and prevents lock-in when partners change. Good leaders reduce friction for their teams the same way good landing pages reduce friction for customers.

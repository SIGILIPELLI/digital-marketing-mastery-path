# 06 · Privacy, Consent & First-Party Data

Marketing runs on data, and the rules around personal data have tightened worldwide. India's **Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023** (with its rules being phased in), Europe's GDPR, and platform changes like browser tracking limits all shape what marketers can collect and use. This module covers compliance basics and how to build a durable **first-party data** strategy. (This is practical guidance, not legal advice.)

## 1. Core privacy principles

1. **Consent** — collect personal data for a specific, stated purpose with clear, informed, unambiguous consent; make withdrawing consent as easy as giving it.
2. **Notice** — explain what you collect, why, how long you keep it and who you share it with, in plain language.
3. **Purpose limitation and minimisation** — collect only what you need and use it only for the stated purpose.
4. **Security** — protect data; have a breach response process.
5. **Rights** — people can access, correct and erase their data and raise grievances.
6. Children's data needs verifiable parental consent and no behavioural tracking or targeted ads at children under the DPDP framework.

## 2. Consent in practice

1. Use a **consent management platform (CMP)** where required (e.g. for EU visitors) to manage cookie consent for analytics and advertising tags.
2. Google's **Consent Mode v2** adjusts Google tags based on consent and models conversions for users who decline; required for using Google ads features with EU/EEA users.
3. Separate consent for marketing emails/WhatsApp from transactional messages; keep records of when and how consent was given.
4. Respect opt-outs instantly across all tools.

## 3. Signal loss and how to adapt

1. Browsers restrict third-party cookies and tracking; iOS App Tracking Transparency limits app tracking; ad blockers reduce measurement.
2. **Server-side tracking** and conversion APIs (Meta Conversions API, Google enhanced conversions) send consented first-party signals more reliably.
3. **Modelled conversions** and MMM (Module 2) fill gaps.
4. Expect platform-reported numbers to be estimates; triangulate.

## 4. Build first-party data

1. Give people reasons to share data willingly: accounts with benefits, loyalty programmes, quizzes, tools, gated guides, community.
2. Unify data in a CRM or **customer data platform (CDP)** with consent status on every record.
3. Use it for personalisation (relevant emails, onsite recommendations), customer-list audiences (hashed uploads, with consent) and lookalikes.
4. Value exchange is the test: if customers wouldn't be happy knowing how you use their data, don't do it.

## Cheat sheet

| Area | Practice |
|---|---|
| Consent | Specific, informed, easy to withdraw |
| Notice | Plain-language privacy policy |
| Minimise | Collect only what's needed |
| Tags | CMP + Consent Mode where required |
| Signals | Conversions API, enhanced conversions |
| First-party data | Value exchange + CRM/CDP with consent |

## How It Actually Works

As third-party tracking declines, the advantage shifts to businesses with direct relationships: data customers give you knowingly, tied to a value exchange, is both more compliant and more accurate than inferred tracking data. Platforms are rebuilding measurement around consented first-party signals and statistical modelling, which is why conversion APIs and clean consent records directly affect ad performance. Privacy compliance and marketing effectiveness now point in the same direction.

Write a short privacy-notice summary in plain language and design a first-party data plan with three value-exchange ideas and how each feeds your CRM with consent status.

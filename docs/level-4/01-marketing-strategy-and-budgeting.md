# 01 · Marketing Strategy & Budgeting

At master level you stop asking "which channel?" and start asking "what must marketing achieve for the business, and what's the best use of each rupee?" This module builds a strategy and a budget you can defend to a founder or CFO.

## 1. Start from business goals

1. Translate the business target into marketing targets: revenue goal ₹1.2 crore → at AOV ₹6,000 that's 2,000 orders → at 30% close rate, 6,700 qualified leads.
2. Split into **new vs returning** customers using historical repeat rates.
3. Identify the constraint: is the problem awareness (few people know you), conversion (they know but don't buy) or retention (they don't come back)?

## 2. Write the strategy

1. **Situation** — market, competitors, current performance, strengths and weaknesses (SWOT).
2. **Target segments and positioning** — who you'll win and why you're the better choice (Module 4).
3. **Objectives** — 3–5 measurable goals for the year.
4. **Strategic choices** — what you'll do *and won't do*: "Win office catering in West Pune through search and LinkedIn; don't chase weddings this year."
5. **Channel roles** — e.g. SEO and content for demand capture and authority, Search ads for high-intent demand, Meta/YouTube for awareness, email/WhatsApp for retention.

## 3. Build the budget

1. **Top-down**: a share of revenue (many small businesses spend roughly 5–12%; fast-growing startups more).
2. **Bottom-up**: target customers × allowable CAC. If CLV is ₹18,000 and you aim for CLV:CAC of 3:1, allowable CAC is ₹6,000 per customer.
3. Reconcile both and keep a **test budget** (10–20%) for new channels and ideas.
4. Include non-media costs: tools, content production, agency/freelancers, people.
5. Phase spend with seasonality (festive peaks, financial-year-end corporate budgets).

## 4. Plan, review, reallocate

1. Quarterly plan with initiatives, owners, budget and KPIs.
2. Monthly review: actual vs plan, channel CAC and marginal returns.
3. **Reallocation rules** written in advance: "If a channel's cost per qualified lead is 30% above target for 6 weeks after optimisation, cut its budget by half and move it to the best performer."
4. Keep a decision log so you learn what worked and why.

## Cheat sheet

| Step | Output |
|---|---|
| Business → marketing targets | Leads, customers, revenue by month |
| Constraint | Awareness, conversion or retention |
| Strategy | Segments, positioning, choices, channel roles |
| Budget | Top-down vs bottom-up + test budget |
| Allowable CAC | CLV ÷ target ratio |
| Governance | Monthly review + reallocation rules |

## How It Actually Works

Budgets follow **diminishing returns**: the first rupees in a channel buy the cheapest, highest-intent customers, and each extra rupee buys slightly more expensive ones. Good allocation equalises the *marginal* return across channels rather than average returns, which is why the best-performing channel on average isn't always where the next rupee should go. Strategy is the discipline of choosing where not to compete, so limited money and attention concentrate where you can actually win.

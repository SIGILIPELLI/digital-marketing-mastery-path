# 08 · Freelancing & Client Management

Many digital marketers work independently — as freelancers or small agencies serving local and global clients. This module covers choosing a niche, pricing, proposals, contracts, delivery and keeping clients happy.

## 1. Choose a niche and offer

1. Specialise by **service** (Google Ads for clinics, SEO for SaaS, Meta ads for D2C brands) or **industry** — specialists win more and charge more.
2. Package services into clear offers: "Local SEO setup + 3 months management", "Meta ads launch for D2C brands".
3. Build proof: case studies (even from practice projects or small first clients), certifications, a portfolio site and LinkedIn presence.

## 2. Find clients

1. Your network and referrals; local business communities.
2. LinkedIn content showing expertise; cold outreach with a personalised mini-audit.
3. Freelance platforms (Upwork, Fiverr, Contra, Toptal) for global clients — compete on specialisation, not lowest price.
4. Partnerships with web developers, designers and agencies who need overflow help.

## 3. Pricing, proposals and contracts

1. Pricing models: hourly, project, monthly retainer, or retainer + performance bonus. Price on value and complexity, not just time.
2. A proposal includes: the client's goal in their words, your diagnosis, scope and deliverables, timeline, price, what you need from them, and success metrics.
3. A contract covers scope, payment terms (advance, milestones), revisions, timelines, confidentiality, account ownership (client owns ad accounts and data), termination notice and liability limits.
4. In India, register for **GST** once turnover crosses the threshold (₹20 lakh for most service providers; ₹10 lakh in special category states) or if you supply services to clients abroad under export rules (LUT for zero-rated exports). Keep proper invoices and track TDS deducted by clients. Get a CA's advice for your situation.

## 4. Delivery and retention

1. Kick-off: goals, access, baseline, communication channel, reporting schedule.
2. Set expectations early: what results are realistic in what time (SEO takes months; ads need a learning period).
3. Regular reporting: short monthly report with results vs goals, what you did, what's next.
4. Communicate proactively about problems; propose solutions.
5. Ask for testimonials and referrals at moments of success; offer expanded services that fit their goals.

## Cheat sheet

| Area | Practice |
|---|---|
| Niche | Service or industry specialisation |
| Offers | Packaged, clear deliverables |
| Leads | Network, LinkedIn, mini-audits, platforms |
| Pricing | Value-based; retainers for stability |

## How It Actually Works

Clients buy reduced risk: confidence that you understand their problem and can deliver. Niching makes that confidence easier to earn — your case studies and language match their situation exactly — and lets you build reusable processes that raise margins. Retainers turn one-off projects into predictable income, but they last only as long as clients see progress, which is why clear expectations and regular, honest reporting matter as much as the work itself.

# 04 · Google Ads: Search Campaigns

Google Search ads put you at the top of results for chosen keywords — immediately — and you pay only when someone clicks. This module walks through building a well-structured search campaign that doesn't waste money.

## 1. Account and campaign setup

1. Create an account at **ads.google.com**. Choose **Expert mode** (switch from the Smart campaign flow) to get full control.
2. Link **GA4** and set up conversion tracking first (Module 8) — without conversions, Google can't optimise and you can't judge results.
3. Create a campaign: goal **Leads** (or Sales), type **Search**.
4. **Networks**: untick Display Network for search campaigns to keep budget on search results.
5. **Locations**: target where customers are (e.g. Pune, or a radius around the café). Under location options choose "Presence: people in or regularly in your targeted locations".
6. **Budget**: start with what you can afford to test for 2–4 weeks (e.g. ₹300–₹500 per day).

## 2. Keywords and match types

1. Build **ad groups** around tight themes: "Office catering", "Party snacks", "Café near me".
2. Match types: **broad** (Google matches related searches — use only with conversion tracking and smart bidding), **phrase** (`"office catering"` — searches including the meaning of the phrase), **exact** (`[office catering pune]` — same meaning as the keyword).
3. Start with phrase and exact for control; add broad later when conversions are flowing.
4. Add **negative keywords** to block wasted clicks: `-jobs -recipe -free -training -course`.
5. Review the **Search terms report** weekly and add irrelevant searches as negatives.

## 3. Ads and assets

1. Write **Responsive Search Ads**: up to 15 headlines (30 characters each) and 4 descriptions (90 characters). Include the keyword, benefit, proof and CTA: "Office Catering in Pune", "Delivered Hot & On Time", "GST Invoice · From ₹120/Head".
2. Add **assets** (formerly extensions): sitelinks (Menu, Pricing, Reviews), callouts ("Same-day orders"), call, location, structured snippets, images.
3. Send traffic to the matching landing page, not the home page.

## 4. Bidding, Quality Score and optimisation

1. New accounts: start with **Maximise clicks** with a max CPC cap, or **Maximise conversions** if tracking is ready. Move to **Target CPA** once you have roughly 15–30 conversions a month.
2. **Quality Score** (1–10, per keyword) reflects expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience. Higher scores lower your cost per click.
3. Weekly routine: check search terms (add negatives), pause poor keywords, test new headlines, adjust bids by device/location/time where data supports it.
4. Judge by **cost per lead and lead quality**, not clicks.

## Cheat sheet

| Setting | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Campaign type | Search, Display Network off |
| Location | Presence in target area |
| Match types | Phrase/exact first; broad with smart bidding |
| Negatives | Add weekly from search terms |
| Ads | RSA: 10–15 headlines, 4 descriptions |
| Assets | Sitelinks, callouts, call, location |
| Bidding | Max clicks/conversions → Target CPA |

## How It Actually Works

Every search triggers an instant **auction** among advertisers whose keywords match. Google ranks ads by **Ad Rank**, which combines your bid with quality (expected CTR, ad relevance, landing page experience) and the expected impact of assets. The actual price you pay is usually less than your maximum bid — roughly what's needed to keep your position — so a more relevant ad can outrank a competitor who bids more while paying less per click. Smart bidding adjusts bids per auction using signals like device, location, time and the searcher's context to predict conversion likelihood.

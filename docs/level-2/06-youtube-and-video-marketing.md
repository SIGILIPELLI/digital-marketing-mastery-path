# 06 · YouTube & Video Marketing

YouTube is the world's second-largest search engine and a huge platform in India. Video builds trust faster than text because people see the product, the place and the person. This module covers channel setup, YouTube SEO, Shorts and measuring what works.

## 1. Channel foundations

1. Create a **brand channel** (not tied to a personal name) in YouTube Studio; add banner, profile image, description with keywords, links and contact email.
2. Create **playlists** for your main topics ("Office snack ideas", "Behind the kitchen", "Customer stories").
3. Add a **channel trailer** for new visitors: who you are, what they'll get, how often you post.

## 2. Plan videos people search for

1. Use YouTube search autocomplete and the **Research** tab in YouTube Studio's analytics to find what your audience looks for.
2. Formats that work for businesses: how-to tutorials, product demos, comparisons, FAQs answered on camera, behind-the-scenes, customer stories.
3. Script the first 15 seconds tightly: state the payoff immediately ("Here are 5 snacks your whole team will love, under ₹150 a head").
4. Good audio matters more than a fancy camera — use a clip-on mic and a quiet room. Phones are fine.

## 3. YouTube SEO

1. **Title** — include the main keyword naturally and make it compelling (around 60 characters).
2. **Thumbnail** — a custom image with a clear face or product and 2–4 large words; it largely decides click-through rate.
3. **Description** — first two lines summarise the video with keywords; add chapters (timestamps like `00:00 Intro`), links and CTA.
4. Add **captions** (auto-generated, then corrected) and choose the right category and language.
5. End screens and cards point viewers to the next video or playlist, increasing session watch time.

## 4. Shorts and measurement

1. **Shorts** (vertical, up to 3 minutes) bring discovery to new audiences; repurpose highlights from long videos and Reels.
2. In YouTube Studio analytics watch: impressions click-through rate, **average view duration**, audience retention graph (where people drop), traffic sources (Search, Suggested, Browse, Shorts feed), and subscribers gained.
3. Improve the first 30 seconds where retention drops most; test new thumbnails on videos with low CTR.
4. Add links to your landing page with UTM parameters (Module 8) to measure leads from YouTube in GA4.

## Cheat sheet

| Element | Best practice |
|---|---|
| Title | Keyword + promise, ~60 chars |
| Thumbnail | Face/product + 2–4 big words |
| First 15s | State the payoff |
| Description | Summary, chapters, links |
| Captions | Always; correct auto captions |
| Analytics | CTR, avg view duration, retention |
| Shorts | Vertical discovery; repurpose highlights |

## How It Actually Works

YouTube's recommendation system aims to maximise viewer satisfaction over time, using signals like click-through rate, watch time, retention, likes, surveys and whether viewers keep watching YouTube afterwards. A high CTR with poor retention signals clickbait and stops being recommended; strong retention tells the system the video delivers. Search traffic is driven by relevance (titles, descriptions, captions), while Suggested and Browse traffic — usually the larger share — depend on how well your videos keep viewers watching.

## Exercise

Plan five YouTube videos for your business with title, thumbnail concept, first-15-second script and description with chapters. Record and publish one (a phone is fine), add captions, and after a week note impressions CTR, average view duration and where retention drops.

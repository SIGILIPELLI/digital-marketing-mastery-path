# 03 · Content Marketing Strategy

Content marketing means publishing genuinely useful material — articles, videos, guides, tools — that attracts your audience, earns trust and leads to sales. This module turns random posting into a strategy with goals, formats and a calendar.

## 1. Content that serves the funnel

1. **Top of funnel (awareness)** — answer broad questions: "15 snack ideas for office meetings", "How much does office catering cost in Pune?".
2. **Middle (consideration)** — comparisons and proof: "Café catering vs aggregators: which is better for a 50-person team?", case studies, menus.
3. **Bottom (conversion)** — pricing pages, FAQs, testimonials, "How to book".
4. Every piece should link naturally to the next step: a guide links to the catering page; a case study ends with a quote CTA.

## 2. Find topics people want

1. Start from your keyword map and **People also ask** questions (Level 1, Module 5).
2. Ask sales or customer-facing staff: "What questions do customers ask before buying?" Each question is a topic.
3. Check Search Console for queries with impressions but low clicks — content you could improve or create.
4. Study competitors' best content (high-ranking pages, most-shared posts) and make something more useful, more current or more specific.

## 3. Create content that's worth ranking

1. Lead with the answer, then add depth (examples, numbers, steps, pictures).
2. Add **first-hand experience**: your own photos, data, prices, mistakes and results — things a generic article can't copy.
3. Use clear structure (H2/H3, lists, tables), a short summary, and internal links.
4. Repurpose: one detailed guide → a carousel, three Reels, an email and a LinkedIn post.
5. Update important pieces at least yearly; refreshed content often recovers lost rankings.

## 4. Plan, publish, measure

1. Build an editorial calendar: topic, target keyword, funnel stage, format, owner, publish date, distribution channels.
2. Consistency beats bursts: one excellent article a week, or two a month, sustained for six months.
3. **Distribute** every piece: email list, social channels, WhatsApp groups, relevant communities.
4. Measure per piece: organic clicks and positions (Search Console), engaged sessions and key events (GA4), backlinks earned, and leads it assisted.

## Cheat sheet

| Stage | Content types | Goal metric |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | How-to guides, lists, short video | Impressions, organic clicks |
| Consideration | Comparisons, case studies, webinars | Engaged time, return visits |
| Conversion | Pricing, FAQs, demos, testimonials | Leads, sales |
| Retention | Tips, newsletters, community | Repeat purchases |

## How It Actually Works

Google's **helpful content** guidance and ranking systems aim to reward content created primarily for people, demonstrating real experience and expertise, and to demote content produced mainly to attract search clicks. Practically, pages that fully satisfy a searcher — so they don't need to go back and click another result — earn better engagement, more links and more shares, which compound over time. Content is an asset: a strong guide can keep bringing traffic for years, unlike an ad that stops the moment you stop paying.

# 08 · E-commerce Marketing

Selling online combines everything in this course with a few e-commerce-specific levers: product pages, marketplaces, average order value and repeat purchase. This module uses an example store, *Leaf & Kettle*, which sells premium tea online across India.

## 1. Core e-commerce metrics

1. **Conversion rate** — orders ÷ sessions (often 1–3% for e-commerce).
2. **AOV (average order value)** — revenue ÷ orders.
3. **CAC (customer acquisition cost)** — marketing spend ÷ new customers.
4. **Repeat purchase rate** and **CLV** — how much a customer is worth over time.
5. **Contribution margin** — revenue minus product cost, shipping, payment fees, returns and ad spend. A sale can lose money if margins are thin.
6. The formula for profitable growth: CLV comfortably above CAC (many aim for 3:1).

## 2. Product pages and the store

1. Product titles with key attributes; benefit-led descriptions; multiple real photos and a short video; size/weight; delivery time and cost upfront.
2. **Reviews and ratings** with photos; Q&A.
3. Trust signals: secure payment, UPI, COD where sensible, easy returns, contact details.
4. Fast mobile checkout: guest checkout, autofill, saved addresses, UPI and wallet options. Platforms like Shopify, WooCommerce, Dukaan or Shiprocket's tools simplify this.
5. Product schema for rich results; Merchant Center feed for Shopping (Module 3).

## 3. Raise AOV and repeat purchases

1. **Bundles** ("Breakfast tea trio"), **free-shipping thresholds** ("Free delivery above ₹799"), cross-sells and upsells at cart.
2. **Subscriptions** for consumables (monthly tea box) to lock in repeat revenue.
3. Post-purchase email/WhatsApp flows: order updates, brewing guide, review request, replenishment reminder after ~30 days.
4. Loyalty points or referral rewards ("Give ₹100, get ₹100").

## 4. Marketplaces and quick commerce

1. **Amazon** and **Flipkart** bring huge demand but charge commissions and fees; optimise listings (titles, A+ content, reviews) and use Sponsored Products ads.
2. Quick-commerce and category platforms (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, Nykaa, Myntra, Meesho) suit certain categories.
3. Balance channels: marketplaces for discovery and volume; your own store (D2C) for margins, customer data and loyalty.
4. Seasonal planning: festive sales (Diwali, Big Billion Days, Great Indian Festival), gifting peaks — prepare stock, creatives and budgets weeks ahead.

## Cheat sheet

| Lever | Metric it moves |
|---|---|
| Better product pages | Conversion rate |
| Bundles, free-shipping threshold | AOV |
| Post-purchase flows, subscriptions | Repeat rate, CLV |
| Shopping/PMax, marketplace ads | New customers (CAC) |
| Reviews with photos | Conversion rate |
| Contribution margin | True profitability |

## How It Actually Works

E-commerce economics are driven by the relationship between what it costs to acquire a customer and what that customer is worth over time. Paid channels often lose money on the first order and earn it back through repeat purchases — which only works if retention, margins and AOV are healthy. That's why strong stores invest as much in post-purchase experience and product quality as in ads: retention improvements raise CLV, which lets you afford more competitive acquisition.

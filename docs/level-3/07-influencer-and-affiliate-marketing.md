# 07 · Influencer & Affiliate Marketing

People trust recommendations from people more than brand ads. **Influencer marketing** borrows a creator's audience and credibility; **affiliate marketing** pays partners only when they drive sales. This module shows how to run both responsibly and measurably.

## 1. Find the right influencers

1. Match **audience**, not follower count: a Pune food creator with 15,000 engaged local followers can outperform a celebrity for a café.
2. Tiers: nano (1k–10k), micro (10k–100k), macro (100k–1M), mega (1M+). Nano and micro often deliver higher engagement and lower cost.
3. Check quality: engagement rate (interactions ÷ followers), comment authenticity, audience location (ask for screenshots of insights), past brand work, brand safety.
4. Find creators via Instagram/YouTube search, hashtags, competitor collaborations, or platforms like Plixxo, Qoruz or Winkl.

## 2. Brief and contract

1. Brief: goal, key message, must-include points (offer, link/code), dos and don'ts, deliverables (1 Reel + 3 Stories), timeline, approval process.
2. Leave creative freedom — creators know what their audience responds to.
3. Agree rights: can you reuse the content in ads (**whitelisting/partnership ads**) and for how long?
4. Compensation: free product/experience (barter), fixed fee, or fee + performance bonus.

## 3. Disclosure and compliance

1. In India, the **ASCI** influencer guidelines and the Department of Consumer Affairs' endorsement guidelines require clear disclosure of material connections: labels like **#ad**, **#collab**, **#sponsored** or "Paid partnership", placed upfront and visible.
2. Use the platform's **Paid partnership** label (Instagram/YouTube).
3. Creators must have genuinely used the product; claims must be truthful. Sensitive categories (health, finance) have extra rules.

## 4. Affiliate programmes

1. Partners (bloggers, coupon sites, creators, comparison sites) promote your product with a unique link or code and earn commission on sales or leads.
2. Run in-house (tools like Refersion, Tapfiliate, or your e-commerce platform's apps) or join networks such as **Amazon Associates** (if you sell on Amazon), Cuelinks, vCommission or Admitad.
3. Set clear terms: commission rate, cookie window (e.g. 30 days), approved promotion methods, banned tactics (brand-keyword bidding, fake coupons).
4. Track every partner with unique links/codes and UTMs; pay on validated sales after the return window.

## Cheat sheet

| Item | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Creator fit | Audience match + engagement, not just followers |
| Brief | Goal, message, deliverables, approval |
| Usage rights | Agree partnership ads/whitelisting |
| Disclosure | #ad / Paid partnership upfront (ASCI) |
| Tracking | Unique codes + UTM links |
| Affiliates | Commission, cookie window, banned tactics |

## How It Actually Works

Influencer posts work because they arrive inside a trusted feed, framed by someone the audience already follows; the endorsement transfers credibility. That trust is also why regulators require disclosure — undisclosed ads mislead consumers. Affiliate marketing aligns incentives by paying only for results, but it also invites gaming (claiming credit for sales that would have happened anyway), so clear rules and attribution windows protect your margins.

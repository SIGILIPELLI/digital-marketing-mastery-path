# 09 · AI Tools for Marketers

AI tools can speed up research, writing, design, analysis and personalisation — if you use them with judgement. This module covers practical workflows, where AI helps most, and the risks you must manage.

## 1. Where AI helps

1. **Research** — summarise competitor pages, cluster keyword lists by intent, extract common objections from reviews.
2. **Ideation and drafting** — content outlines, ad headline variations, email subject lines, social captions, video scripts.
3. **Design and video** — image generation and editing (Canva's AI features, Adobe Firefly), video editing and captioning (CapCut, Descript), resizing creatives for every placement.
4. **Analysis** — explain trends in exported data, write spreadsheet formulas, draft report commentary.
5. **Customer interaction** — website chat assistants and WhatsApp bots that answer FAQs and capture leads, with a human hand-off.
6. **Ad platforms' built-in AI** — Google's automatically created assets, Meta Advantage+ creative and audiences.

## 2. Prompting that produces useful output

1. Give **context**: business, persona, offer, tone, channel and constraints.
2. Ask for **specific formats**: "15 headlines under 30 characters, each with a different angle: price, speed, quality, proof".
3. Provide **examples** of good past content.
4. **Iterate**: ask for critique ("Which three are strongest for an office admin and why?"), then refine.
5. Example prompt: *"You are a performance marketer. Our café in Baner caters office snacks for 20–100 people, from ₹120/head, GST invoice, delivered hot. Persona: office admin who fears late or cold food. Write 10 Google Ads headlines (≤30 characters) and 4 descriptions (≤90 characters) emphasising reliability and proof."*

## 3. Quality, accuracy and brand

1. **Fact-check** everything: AI can state wrong prices, laws or statistics confidently.
2. Add **first-hand experience**, examples and data — this is what makes content valuable and distinct.
3. Keep a **brand voice guide** (tone, words to use/avoid) and include it in prompts.
4. Edit for local relevance (Indian pricing, festivals, language mix where appropriate).

## 4. Risks and responsible use

1. **Privacy** — don't paste customer personal data into tools that may store or train on it; check settings and company policy (DPDP Act obligations).
2. **Copyright and likeness** — avoid generating content imitating real people, brands or copyrighted characters; check usage rights for generated images.
3. **Disclosure** — be transparent where AI-generated content could mislead (e.g. synthetic testimonials are never acceptable).
4. **Search quality** — publishing mass AI content with little value risks demotion (Module 2).
5. Keep a human accountable for every published piece.

## Cheat sheet

| Task | AI use | Human role |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword clustering | Group by intent | Validate with SERPs |
| Ad copy | Generate variations | Pick, edit, test |
| Content | Outline, draft | Add expertise, fact-check |
| Creative | Resize, edit, captions | Brand check |
| Analysis | Explain data | Verify, decide |
| Chatbots | FAQs, lead capture | Escalation, review |

## How It Actually Works

Large language models predict plausible text based on patterns in their training data and the context you provide; they don't inherently know your business or verify facts. That's why rich context produces dramatically better output, and why accuracy needs human checking. In marketing, AI shifts the bottleneck from producing drafts to judging quality — the marketers who benefit most are those with clear strategy, strong taste and good testing discipline.

# 01 · Link Building & Digital PR

Links from other reputable websites are still one of the strongest signals of authority in search. This module shows ethical ways to earn them — by creating things worth referencing and telling the right people — and the tactics to avoid.

## 1. What makes a link valuable

1. **Relevance** — a link from a Pune food blog or a business magazine matters more to Chai Corner than one from an unrelated site.
2. **Authority and trust** — sites that are themselves well-linked and real (news, universities, industry bodies).
3. **Placement** — an editorial link in the body of an article beats a footer or directory listing.
4. **Anchor text** — natural, descriptive anchors ("office catering in Pune", the brand name); over-optimised exact-match anchors look manipulative.
5. `rel="nofollow"`, `"sponsored"` and `"ugc"` links pass less or no ranking credit, but still bring traffic and brand exposure.

## 2. Linkable assets

1. **Original data** — "We analysed 500 office orders: the most popular meeting snacks in Pune." Journalists and bloggers cite data.
2. **Tools and calculators** — a catering cost calculator, a GST invoice template.
3. **Definitive guides** — the best resource on a topic in your niche.
4. **Local resources** — "Guide to the best co-working spaces in Baner".
5. Publish the asset on your site and make it easy to cite (summary stats, embeddable charts with credit).

## 3. Outreach and digital PR

1. Build a list of relevant sites: who has linked to similar resources (check competitors' backlinks in Ahrefs/Semrush free tools), local media, niche bloggers, newsletter writers.
2. Personalise every pitch: why this asset helps *their* readers, one short paragraph, no attachments.
3. Respond to journalist requests on platforms like **Qwoted** or **Featured**, and on social media (#journorequest).
4. **Unlinked mentions**: find articles mentioning your brand without a link (Google Alerts) and politely ask for one.
5. **Broken link building**: find dead links on relevant pages and suggest your resource as a replacement.
6. Partnerships: co-host events, sponsor local meetups, contribute expert quotes or guest articles to genuinely relevant publications.

## 4. What to avoid

1. Buying links that pass ranking credit, link exchanges at scale, private blog networks and spammy guest-post farms violate Google's spam policies and can lead to manual actions or ignored links.
2. Mass directory submissions and comment spam add no value.
3. If you inherit a manual action, clean up links and use the **Disavow tool** only for clearly manipulative links you can't remove.

## Cheat sheet

| Tactic | Effort | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Original data study | High | Very high |
| Tools/templates | Medium | High |
| Journalist requests | Medium | High |
| Unlinked mentions | Low | Medium |
| Broken link building | Medium | Medium |
| Local partnerships | Low–medium | Medium (local) |
| Paid links / PBNs | — | Risky, violates policy |

## How It Actually Works

Google's original insight was that links act like citations: a page linked by many trusted pages is probably trustworthy itself. Modern systems are far more sophisticated — they weigh relevance, context and patterns, and they're trained to ignore or penalise manipulative links — but the principle holds. That's why earned editorial links compound: each one raises the authority of the whole site, helping every page rank, while bought links are a liability that can be discounted overnight.
